ASSIGNMENT 1: PROBABILITY & STATISTICS - MCQ ANSWERS
====================================================

1. Probability Does Not Guarantee Outcomes
   Answer: C
   Explanation: Probability describes likelihood, not guaranteed outcomes. With 10 sensors and 20% failure rate, we might see 0, 1, 2, 3, or more failures. The expected value is 2, but that doesn't guarantee exactly 2 will fail.

2. Distribution Shape
   Answer: B
   Explanation: A normal distribution is symmetric and bell-shaped, with most values concentrated near the mean and tails tapering off symmetrically.

3. Standard Deviation
   Answer: C
   Explanation: Standard deviation measures how spread out data is from the mean. A smaller standard deviation indicates data points are closer to the mean, while a larger one indicates more spread.

4. Outliers and Statistics
   Answer: D
   Explanation: Extreme values (outliers) can significantly affect the mean and standard deviation, potentially skewing statistical measures. The median and mode are more robust to outliers.

5. Sensor Failure Probability
   Answer: B
   Explanation: With three independent sensors, the probability that all three fail is: P(all fail) = 0.1 × 0.1 × 0.1 = 0.001 or 0.1%

6. Conditional Probability
   Answer: B
   Explanation: P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B|A). If events are independent: P(both fail) = 0.15 × 0.15 = 0.0225 or 2.25%

7. Data Variability
   Answer: C
   Explanation: Data with higher variability (more spread) typically has a larger standard deviation compared to data tightly clustered around the mean.

8. Normal Distribution Properties
   Answer: B
   Explanation: In a normal distribution: approximately 68% of data falls within 1 standard deviation, 95% within 2 standard deviations, and 99.7% within 3 standard deviations.

9. Confidence Intervals
   Answer: C
   Explanation: A 95% confidence interval means if we repeated the experiment many times, approximately 95% of the intervals would contain the true population parameter.

10. Hypothesis Testing
    Answer: A
    Explanation: A p-value less than 0.05 is typically considered statistically significant in hypothesis testing, suggesting strong evidence against the null hypothesis.

11. Statistical Significance vs Practical Significance
    Answer: D
    Explanation: A result can be statistically significant (unlikely due to random chance) but not practically significant (the actual effect size is too small to matter in practice).

12. Correlation and Causation
    Answer: B
    Explanation: Correlation measures association between variables, but does not imply causation. Other factors (confounders) might explain the observed relationship.

13. Mean vs Median
    Answer: C
    Explanation: When data has extreme outliers, the median is more representative of the "typical" value than the mean, which is pulled by extreme values.

14. Probability of Event or Its Complement
    Answer: D
    Explanation: P(A or not A) = 1, since an event either occurs or doesn't. This is a fundamental rule of probability.

15. Independent Events
    Answer: B
    Explanation: If two events are independent, knowing one occurred doesn't change the probability of the other. P(B|A) = P(B).

16. Sample Size Effect
    Answer: A
    Explanation: Larger sample sizes generally provide more reliable estimates of population parameters and reduce the standard error.

17. Type I Error
    Answer: A
    Explanation: Type I error (false positive) occurs when we reject a true null hypothesis. The significance level (α) controls this probability.

18. Type II Error
    Answer: B
    Explanation: Type II error (false negative) occurs when we fail to reject a false null hypothesis. This is related to statistical power.

19. Statistical Power
    Answer: D
    Explanation: Statistical power is the probability of correctly rejecting a false null hypothesis. Higher power is better and depends on sample size, effect size, and significance level.

20. Variance and Standard Deviation
    Answer: A
    Explanation: Variance is the square of standard deviation. If std dev = 5, then variance = 25.

21. Covariance
    Answer: C
    Explanation: Covariance measures how two variables change together. A positive covariance indicates they tend to increase together; negative means opposite movement.

22. Correlation Coefficient
    Answer: B
    Explanation: Correlation ranges from -1 to +1, where -1 is perfect negative correlation, 0 is no correlation, and +1 is perfect positive correlation.

23. Central Limit Theorem
    Answer: C
    Explanation: The Central Limit Theorem states that the distribution of sample means approaches a normal distribution as sample size increases, regardless of the original distribution.

24. Bayes' Theorem Application
    Answer: A
    Explanation: Bayes' theorem relates conditional probabilities: P(A|B) = P(B|A) × P(A) / P(B). It's useful for updating probabilities with new information.

25. Predictive vs Descriptive Statistics
    Answer: B
    Explanation: Descriptive statistics summarize data (mean, median, std dev), while inferential/predictive statistics make predictions about populations from samples.
